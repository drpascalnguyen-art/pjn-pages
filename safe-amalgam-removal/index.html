Silver (amalgam) fillings

Thinking about removing your silver fillings? Do it safely.

Silver fillings are about 50% mercury. If you and your dentist decide to replace them, how they're removed matters as much as what replaces them.

What the FDA says

In 2020 the FDA recommended that some people avoid getting new amalgam fillings when appropriate: pregnant or nursing women, women planning a pregnancy, children under 6, and people with kidney problems, neurological conditions or a known mercury sensitivity.

The FDA does not recommend removing healthy, intact fillings just because they're amalgam, since removal itself can release mercury vapor. So the decision is personal, made with your dentist, based on the condition of the fillings and your health.

How we remove amalgam: the SMART protocol

When removal is the right call, we follow the SMART protocol (Safe Mercury Amalgam Removal Technique), designed to reduce mercury exposure for you and our team:

  • A protective dental dam to isolate the tooth
  • High-volume suction right at the tooth to capture vapor and particles
  • Removing the filling in larger chunks rather than grinding it away
  • Fresh air supply and protective covering for you
  • Room air filtration
  • Replacement with metal-free materials, chosen with optional biocompatibility testing

Common questions

Should I remove all my fillings at once?

Usually not. We plan removal in stages based on which fillings are failing, cracked or decayed, and on your health. We'll map it out together.

What replaces the amalgam?

Tooth-colored, metal-free materials. For larger fillings, that may be a ceramic inlay, onlay or crown so the tooth stays strong.

Will removing my fillings fix my health problems?

We can't promise that, and you should be wary of anyone who does. What we can do is remove failing fillings carefully, replace them with metal-free materials, and look at your oral health as part of your whole health.

What does it cost?

It depends on how many fillings, their size and what replaces them. You'll get a written estimate after your exam.
