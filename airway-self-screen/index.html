Why airway matters

In obstructive sleep apnea, the airway narrows or collapses during sleep and breathing pauses. Oxygen drops, and the body wakes just enough to breathe, often many times a night without you remembering. Over time, untreated sleep apnea is linked to high blood pressure, heart disease and diabetes.

Dentists often spot the early signs first: worn teeth from grinding, a scalloped tongue, a narrow palate or mouth breathing.

What we do

  • An airway screening as part of every new patient exam, including a 3D scan of the airway
  • Help arranging a home sleep test. Only a sleep study can diagnose sleep apnea
  • Oral appliance therapy for appropriate patients, coordinated with your physician
  • For kids: airway-focused growth guidance, myofunctional therapy and tongue-tie evaluation
