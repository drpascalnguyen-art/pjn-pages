Root canal second opinion

Told you need a root canal, or worried about one you have?

Not every root canal-treated tooth needs to come out, and not every one should stay. A second opinion looks at the whole picture before you decide.

Your options, side by side

Keep it

Some teeth heal well and can be kept. Sometimes a retreatment or a referral to an endodontist is the best path.

Remove it carefully

If a treated tooth shows ongoing infection or inflammation, careful removal with thorough cleaning of the site, ozone and PRF to support healing may be best.

Replace it

After removal, options include a metal-free zirconia implant, a bridge or a partial, depending on your bone and bite.

What we look at

  • A 3D (CBCT) scan, which shows infection and bone loss that flat 2D x-rays can miss
  • Your symptoms, and how the tooth and gums look and feel
  • Your overall health history: chronic inflammation or infection can be linked to oral health
  • Your goals and how you feel about each option

Common questions

My root canal doesn't hurt. Could there still be a problem?

Sometimes, yes. Infection around a treated tooth can be silent. A 3D scan is the best way to check.

Do you just pull every root canal tooth?

No. Our job is to give you a clear, balanced picture. Many patients leave keeping their tooth.

Do I need my old x-rays?

If you have them, bring or send them. They help us compare over time. We'll take our own 3D scan either way.
